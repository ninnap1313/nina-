OpenFinance Hub: all banks, one window

An AI financial-health app built on Open Banking. It brings accounts, cards, loans, deposits, savings and pension from every bank into one place and helps people make better money decisions. Read-only by design: it never moves the user's money.

RoleProduct / UI-UX Designer
PlatformsiOS / Android app, desktop web, organisation panel
ScopeResearch, flows, prototype, design system
ContextMVP demo for a GITA competition
22mobile screens, from onboarding to profile
3surfaces: mobile, desktop and organisation web panel
0money movements: the app only reads, with consent

The problem

People in Georgia often hold products in several banks. To see their real picture they open three or four apps, and banks offer products without seeing the whole situation. “Banks offer us products. OpenFinance Hub tells us which ones we actually need.”

Onboarding & consent

A demo mode lets people look around before sharing anything. Registration asks only for the basics, and the consent screen explains each data source (Open Banking, Creditinfo, RS.ge) in plain words with a separate switch, so the user stays in control of what is shared.

Financial health at a glance

The home screen opens with the credit limit, goals and a 0–100 Financial Health Score. The score breaks down into clear factors with a colour scale from poor to excellent. Spending is grouped by category against personal limits, and receipts can be added with a photo.

Accounts, loans and deposits

All banks and accounts in one list, loans with remaining balance and next payment, and a loan profile that compares offers by full cost, not just the headline rate. Status chips and consistent cards keep long financial data easy to scan.

AI navigator, goals and rewards

An AI financial navigator answers questions such as “Can I afford this?” using the user's real data. Goals turn saving into visible progress, and points and badges reward healthy habits.

Responsive desktop

The same product on desktop: a sidebar replaces the bottom bar, and cards reflow into wider layouts, so the experience stays consistent across platforms.

Organisation web panel

Banks and lenders sign in with 2FA and see only the profiles that users chose to share with them. They review a loan profile, send an offer that appears inside the app, and every action is recorded in an audit log.

Design system

Indigo for actions and AI, emerald for positive money, and a red, orange and yellow scale reserved for warnings. Glass cards on a calm background, Noto Sans Georgian for text and a monospace face for figures. Built for light and dark mode, with colour never carrying meaning alone.

GSG: one move ahead

A corporate website and reusable design system for GSG, a technology-enabled BPO and IT partner in Tbilisi serving global brands in customer experience, business process management and software engineering.

RoleUX/UI Designer: research, IA, visual design, design system, handoff
ScopeMarketing website, desktop + mobile
ToolsFigma, FigJam, Figma Prototyping
Deliverables13 templates, component library, dev handoff
13page templates designed
1440 / 375breakpoints, each drawn as its own layout
16shared components in the library

The challenge

The company had outgrown its old site: an outdated, text-heavy page that read like a local vendor rather than an international outsourcing partner. An audit against how the business actually sells surfaced four gaps, and each became a design decision.

01Read like a local vendorRepositioned around one promise, “One Move Ahead”, with an Impact in Numbers band above the fold.
02Three services in one flat listCX, BPM and IT became colour-coded verticals, each with its own card, entry point and detail template.
03Hiring with no funnelCareers became a first-class section with an inline application, plus an “Our Space” culture page.
04Every page hand-builtA component library with type ramp, colour tokens, cards, navigation and footer, shipped as a documented handoff page.

Information architecture

Thirteen templates, six top-level destinations. Every service vertical resolves in one click; legal and error states were specified up front. Two primary flows: sales (to “Leave a request”) and recruiting (to “Apply”).

Design system

The tokens came first and every page was assembled from them. Violet, lime and cream mark the three verticals on a near-black surface; Quinn Bold for display, Aloevera Display for UI and body; a 12-column desktop grid and 4-column mobile grid.

Homepage

The whole proposition in one scroll: who GSG is, the numbers that prove it, the three things they sell, and two live content streams that keep the page from going stale.

Colour as wayfinding

Each vertical has a colour and a folder-tab card. That colour follows the visitor into the detail page, the navigation state and the request form, so a returning visitor knows at a glance they are in the right place.

Content that keeps working

News and careers run without a designer: one card component holds every article, and a job listing becomes an application in two screens, with no third-party board.

Designed twice, not squeezed

Every template was drawn at 375 px as its own layout. The proof band moves above the service cards, the folder cards stack, and the desktop CTA rail becomes a fixed bottom bar.

What shipped

Thirteen templates, ready to build, desktop and mobile, on a handoff page with each template marked done, in review or pending. A library, not a mockup: new pages are assembled from stock. A structure the client can run: the team publishes news and roles without touching layout.

CT LAB: tracking medicine in clinical trials

A platform for clinical research associates to follow trial kits from warehouse to hospital site: what is ready, in transit, expiring or below safety stock, and what to order or reconcile next.

RoleUI/UX Designer
ContextEPAM design challenge
ToolsFigma
DeliverablesUI kit + 7 product areas

Overview

Clinical trials depend on the right medication reaching the right site before it expires. The design gives a study coordinator one place to see stock health across sites and act on it: order, reconcile or check a kit.

This was a design challenge for EPAM, so it is a concept: there was no access to trial staff for research.

Dashboard

Five numbers answer “is anything wrong?” first: ready, in transit, expiring soon, expired and below safety stock. Below them, each site card shows its status chip, stock split and safety-stock bar, with View kits, Order and Reconcile as direct actions.

Warehouse

Tabs split 150 kits by state (available, pre-expiry, expired, waste), and status chips use the same colours as the dashboard. Reconciliation starts from one primary button.

Orders

Filter by site, delivery date, status, location, requester or supplier. In transit, pending, cancelled and delivered each get their own chip so a long table can be scanned by colour.
